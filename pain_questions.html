1. Pain that lasts longer than 3 months and often impacts the patient's quality of life  
is deﬁned as:  
A) Acute pain  
B) Chronic pain  
C) Referred pain  
D) Breakthrough pain  
 Hint: The timeline of three months is the standard clinical threshold separating acute from this  
type.  
2. Which type of pain arises directly from a lesion or disease aﬀecting the  
somatosensory nervous system, often described as 'burning' or 'electric'?  
A) Neuropathic pain  
B) Nociceptive visceral pain  
C) Somatic pain  
D) Acute inﬂammatory pain  
 Hint: This involves actual damage to the nerves themselves, causing misﬁring signals.  
3. A sharp, sudden pain resulting directly from recent tissue damage (e.g., a  
sprained ankle) lasting less than 3 months is:  
A) Mixed pain  
B) Chronic pain  
C) Acute nociceptive pain  
D) Central sensitization  
 Hint: It serves as a protective warning mechanism for the body to heal the injury.

4. Pain originating from deep internal organs (e.g., gallstones) is classiﬁed as a  
subtype of nociceptive pain known as:  
A) Neuropathic  
B) Somatic  
C) Central  
D) Visceral  
 Hint: This subtype is often poorly localized and can feel like a deep, dull ache.  
5. Which ﬁbers are responsible for transmitting fast, sharp, well-localized acute pain  
signals to the spinal cord?  
A) C-ﬁbers  
B) A-delta ﬁbers  
C) B-ﬁbers  
D) Parasympathetic ﬁbers  
 Hint: These ﬁbers are myelinated, allowing the pain signal to travel very quickly.  
6. Which unmyelinated ﬁbers transmit slow, dull, aching, and poorly localized  
continuous pain?  
A) C-ﬁbers  
B) A-beta ﬁbers  
C) Motor neurons  
D) A-delta ﬁbers  
 Hint: Because they lack a myelin sheath, signals travel much slower through these ﬁbers.  
7. The physiological process where a noxious stimulus (like a burn) is converted into  
an electrical signal at the nerve ending is called:  
A) Transmission  
B) Modulation  
C) Transduction  
D) Perception  
 Hint: Think of it as translating a physical injury into the 'language' of the nervous system.

8. During pain transmission, the electrical signal travels from the peripheral nerve up  
to which structure before reaching the brain?  
A) The spinal cord (dorsal horn)  
B) The heart  
C) The adrenal gland  
D) The liver  
 Hint: This structure acts as the main highway and sorting station for bodily sensations.  
9. In the context of the pain pathway, what does the brain do during the 'Modulation'  
phase?  
A) It completely disconnects the nerves  
B) It sends inhibitory or excitatory signals down the spinal cord to alter pain intensity  
C) It causes the injury to bleed more  
D) It shuts down all sensory input  
 Hint: The brain can release endorphins to 'turn down the volume' of the pain signal.  
10. Which inﬂammatory mediators are produced locally at the site of tissue injury  
and are the primary targets of NSAIDs?  
A) Histamine and Dopamine  
B) Serotonin and Melatonin  
C) Adrenaline and Noradrenaline  
D) Prostaglandins and Bradykinin  
 Hint: These chemicals sensitize nerve endings and cause localized swelling and redness.

A) Overdose  
B) Onset (When and how did the pain start?)  
C) Origin (What country did you travel to?)  
D) Observation  
 Hint: Knowing if the pain started suddenly or gradually is key to diagnosis.

12. Which question directly addresses the 'Character' (C) component of the  
SOCRATES assessment?  
A) Is the pain moving anywhere else?  
B) What number is your pain on a scale of 0 to 10?  
C) What does the pain feel like (e.g., sharp, dull, throbbing)?  
D) Does resting make the pain better?  
 Hint: This asks the patient to describe the actual physical sensation of the pain.  
13. The 'R' in SOCRATES stands for Radiation. Which condition is a classic example  
of radiating pain that might present at a pharmacy?  
A) Sciatica (lower back pain shooting down the leg)  
B) A simple paper cut on the ﬁnger  
C) A mild tension headache  
D) A superﬁcial skin abrasion  
 Hint: Nerve root compression in the spine often causes pain to travel along the nerve pathway.  
14. Why is relying solely on the WWHAM mnemonic insuﬃcient for a thorough pain  
assessment?  
A) WWHAM is only used for children  
B) WWHAM takes too much time to ask  
C) It lacks detailed symptom characterization tools needed to assess pain severity and type  
D) It is illegal to use in a pharmacy  
 Hint: WWHAM is good for general triage but doesn't drill down into the speciﬁc qualities of the  
pain sensation.

15. Which symptom in an older adult (>50 years) with new-onset back pain is  
considered a 'Red Flag' in the TUNAFISH mnemonic?  
A) Pain that improves with rest  
B) Unexplained weight loss  
C) A history of mild indigestion  
D) Pain limited strictly to one muscle after gardening  
 Hint: This symptom is highly suspicious for hidden malignancies (cancer).  
16. What does the 'T' in the TUNAFISH red ﬂag mnemonic stand for?  
A) Trauma (e.g., a fall or car accident)  
B) Timing  
C) Temperature  
D) Toxicity  
 Hint: Sudden severe pain following a physical impact requires medical imaging to rule out  
fractures.  
17. A history of which condition (the 'H' in TUNAFISH) combined with new, severe  
pain requires urgent referral?  
A) History of common colds  
B) History of tension headaches  
C) History of cancer  
D) History of mild allergies  
 Hint: There is always a high risk that the disease has metastasized (spread) to the bones or  
spine.

18. A patient presents with back pain, high fever, and a history of IV drug use. Why is  
this a Red Flag?  
A) They might be dehydrated  
B) High risk of spinal infection (e.g., epidural abscess or osteomyelitis)  
C) They likely just pulled a muscle  
D) The fever is completely unrelated  
 Hint: Needles can introduce bacteria directly into the bloodstream, which can settle in the spinal  
column.  
19. Paracetamol is classiﬁed clinically as an analgesic and an antipyretic. What  
crucial property does it LACK compared to Ibuprofen?  
A) It lacks pain-relieving abilities  
B) It lacks fever-reducing abilities  
C) It lacks the ability to cross the blood-brain barrier  
D) It lacks clinically signiﬁcant peripheral anti-inﬂammatory eﬀects  
 Hint: It will not reduce swelling in a sprained ankle as eﬀectively as an NSAID.  
20. What is the maximum safe daily dose of oral Paracetamol for a healthy adult to  
prevent hepatotoxicity?  
A) 1 gram  
B) 2 grams  
C) 4 grams (e.g., 8 x 500mg tablets)  
D) 8 grams  
 Hint: Exceeding this limit overwhelms the liver's glutathione stores, leading to toxic buildup.

21. Which patient group is at the HIGHEST risk for paracetamol-induced  
hepatotoxicity even at standard maximum doses?  
A) Teenagers playing sports  
B) Patients with chronic heavy alcohol use or malnutrition  
C) Pregnant women in their ﬁrst trimester  
D) Patients with mild asthma  
 Hint: These conditions deplete the liver enzymes needed to safely metabolize the drug.  
22. How do Non-Steroidal Anti-Inﬂammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) like Diclofenac primarily  
reduce pain?  
A) By inhibiting Cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, thus reducing prostaglandin synthesis  
B) By acting as direct agonists on mu-opioid receptors  
C) By blocking sodium channels in peripheral nerves  
D) By increasing histamine release  
 Hint: They block the chemical factory that produces the molecules responsible for inﬂammation.  
23. What is the maximum duration an adult should use OTC oral NSAIDs  
continuously without a doctor's supervision?  
A) 1 day  
B) 2 weeks  
C) 3 to 5 days  
D) 1 month  
 Hint: Short-term use minimizes the cumulative risk of gastric ulcers and kidney strain.  
24. Oral NSAIDs carry a well-documented risk of causing adverse events in which  
two primary organ systems?  
A) Lungs and brain  
B) Pancreas and gallbladder  
C) Gastrointestinal tract (bleeding/ulcers) and Kidneys (impairment)  
D) Skin and eyes  
 Hint: Prostaglandins protect the stomach lining and maintain blood ﬂow to the renal system.

25. Why are oral NSAIDs generally contraindicated in patients with severe heart  
failure or a recent myocardial infarction?  
A) They cause sudden weight loss  
B) They can cause ﬂuid retention and increase the risk of thrombotic cardiovascular events  
C) They permanently lower blood pressure  
D) They dissolve blood clots too quickly  
 Hint: Inhibition of certain COX enzymes disrupts the balance of prostacyclin and thromboxane,  
straining the heart.  
26. What is the primary advantage of recommending a Topical NSAID (e.g.,  
Diclofenac gel) over oral ibuprofen for localized knee osteoarthritis?  
A) It delivers the drug directly to the joint with minimal systemic absorption, vastly reducing  
GI and renal risks  
B) It cures the arthritis permanently within 2 days  
C) It works systemically on all joints simultaneously  
D) It is much cheaper  
 Hint: The drug stays where it is applied, avoiding the stomach and bloodstream.  
27. When calculating the dose of an OTC liquid analgesic (like paracetamol  
suspension) for a child, what is the most accurate parameter to use?  
A) The child's age in months  
B) The child's height  
C) The child's body weight in kilograms  
D) The child's gender  
 Hint: Age is an estimate; actual physical size determines how the drug is distributed in the body.

28. In geriatric patients (the elderly), why is Paracetamol often the preferred ﬁrst-line  
analgesic over oral NSAIDs?

B) Paracetamol is stronger than morphine in the elderly  
C) The elderly have a much higher risk of NSAID-induced GI bleeding and renal failure  
D) NSAIDs cause immediate hearing loss in older patients  
 Hint: Aging naturally reduces kidney function and thins the stomach lining, making NSAIDs

pregnancy?  
A) Paracetamol  
B) Codeine  
C) Topical menthol creams  
D) All NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen)  
 Hint: These drugs can cause premature closure of a vital blood vessel in the baby's heart  
(ductus arteriosus).

experiencing a mild tension headache?  
A) Naproxen  
B) Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)  
C) High-dose Aspirin  
D) Diclofenac potassium  
 Hint: It has decades of safety data showing no teratogenic eﬀects when used at standard doses.

cold/ﬂu remedies simultaneously?

standard tablets

32. If an adult patient has uncontrolled hypertension and requires OTC pain relief for  
a headache, which option poses the least risk of spiking their blood pressure?  
A) Oral Ibuprofen  
B) Oral Diclofenac  
C) Oral Paracetamol  
D) Oral Naproxen  
 Hint: NSAIDs cause ﬂuid retention which opposes anti-hypertensive drugs; this alternative does  
not.  
33. Which non-pharmacological therapy is highly recommended in the ﬁrst 24-48  
hours after an acute soft tissue injury (e.g., sprained wrist)?

B) Heat therapy (Hot water bottle) to increase blood ﬂow  
C) Vigorous physical massage directly on the injury  
D) Complete immobilization in a heavy cast for a month  
 Hint: Cold constricts blood vessels, limiting the initial rush of inﬂammatory ﬂuid into the tissue.

therapy provides better symptomatic relief?  
A) Ice baths

C) Absolute bed rest  
D) Keeping the area cold and wet

35. What is meant by the term 'Hyperalgesia' in the context of pain pathophysiology?

C) An exaggerated, unusually severe pain response to a mildly painful stimulus  
D) Hallucinating pain that isn't there  
 Hint: The preﬁx 'Hyper-' means over or excessive; the nervous system overreacts to the trigger.

A) Analgesia

 Hint: This is a classic sign of severe neuropathic sensitization or nerve injury.

37. A patient asks for OTC ibuprofen for a toothache. During WWHAM, they reveal  
they take Warfarin (a blood thinner). What is the appropriate pharmacist action?

B) Refuse the sale and recommend paracetamol; NSAIDs severely increase bleeding risk  
with Warfarin  
C) Tell them to double their Warfarin dose

medication MUST BE AVOIDED due to the risk of Reye's Syndrome?

C) Aspirin  
D) Oral rehydration salts  
 Hint: This speciﬁc drug triggers a rare but often fatal liver and brain swelling in children with viral  
infections.

B) Mild headache  
C) Menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhea)  
D) Muscle strains  
 Hint: Nerve pain often requires prescription drugs like Gabapentin or Amitriptyline to alter nerve  
conduction.

A) Swelling

41. A patient with asthma requests OTC ibuprofen. Why must the pharmacist  
proceed with caution?  
A) Ibuprofen causes lung cancer  
B) Approximately 10-20% of asthmatics have NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease  
(aspirin-sensitive asthma)  
C) It permanently destroys the inhaler's eﬀectiveness  
D) It causes immediate pneumonia  
 Hint: Blocking the COX pathway in these patients shunts arachidonic acid to the lipoxygenase  
pathway, causing severe bronchospasm.  
42. Which patient behavior is considered the cornerstone of 'Safety Netting' in

B) Taking the medication with food  
C) Knowing exactly when and why to escalate care to a doctor if symptoms worsen or fail to  
improve  
D) Returning the empty box to the pharmacy  
 Hint: It ensures the patient isn't left in the dark if the initial OTC treatment fails or red ﬂags  
emerge.

43. Topical rubefacients (like deep heat creams) provide pain relief primarily through  
which mechanism?

underlying pain)  
C) Rebuilding torn cartilage  
D) Freezing the nerve endings completely  
 Hint: They trick the brain by ﬂooding the local nerves with harmless sensory data, overriding the

A) Oral Ibuprofen  
B) Oral Naproxen  
C) Oral Paracetamol  
D) High-dose Codeine  
 Hint: This is universally recognized as the safest analgesic across the entirety of gestation.

A) Tension-type headache

D) Brain tumor

induced.

(menstrual cramps) due to its speciﬁc mechanism?

B) NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen or Naproxen)  
C) Topical Diclofenac  
D) Glucosamine

A) Aspirin 300mg

D) Naproxen 250mg
