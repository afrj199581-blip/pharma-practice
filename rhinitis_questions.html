1. What is the simplest deﬁnition of rhinitis?  
A) Inﬂammation and swelling of the mucous membrane inside the nose  
B) A bacterial infection of the lungs  
C) The formation of polyps in the nasal cavity  
D) A fungal infection of the throat  
 Hint: It is considered a chronic inﬂammatory upper airway disease.  
2. Which of the following is NOT one of the classic four symptoms of rhinitis?  
A) Frequent sneezing  
B) High fever  
C) Nasal congestion  
D) Rhinorrhea (runny nose)  
 Hint: This symptom is more typical of a systemic infection like the ﬂu, not standard rhinitis.  
3. Type 1 Rhinitis (Allergic Rhinitis) is mediated by which of the following?  
A) IgG response  
B) IgM response  
C) IgE response to aeroallergens  
D) IgA response  
 Hint: This antibody is primarily responsible for allergic reactions and sensitizing mast cells.

rhinitis (Hay fever)?  
A) Dust mites  
B) Mold  
C) Pet dander  
D) Outdoor pollens, trees, and grasses

5. What primarily characterizes Type 2 Non-Allergic Rhinitis (NAR)?  
A) Absence of histamine involvement  
B) Overuse of nasal sprays  
C) Bacterial infection  
D) High levels of IgE  
 Hint: It involves nasal reactivity to wind, smoke, or temperature changes, but lacks the typical  
allergic pathway.  
6. Vasomotor rhinitis, a type of non-allergic rhinitis, is typically triggered by what?  
A) Eating spicy food  
B) Temperature changes, strong odors, and smoke  
C) Pollen exposure  
D) Viral pathogens  
 Hint: This condition involves hypersensitivity of the nervous system to environmental factors, not  
allergens.  
7. Which type of rhinitis occurs speciﬁcally after eating spicy or hot foods?  
A) Vasomotor rhinitis  
B) Infective rhinitis  
C) Gustatory rhinitis  
D) Rhinitis medicamentosa  
 Hint: The name relates to the sense of taste and eating.

8. Rhinitis medicamentosa is a rebound congestion caused by the chronic overuse  
of which medications?  
A) Oral antihistamines  
B) Corticosteroid sprays  
C) Saline irrigations  
D) Topical decongestants (e.g., oxymetazoline)

9. What is the recommended treatment approach to reverse Rhinitis  
medicamentosa?  
A) Nasal steroid and saline spray  
B) Increasing the dose of the decongestant  
C) Oral antibiotics  
D) Immediate surgery  
 Hint: You need to reduce inﬂammation safely while ﬂushing the nasal passages.

common condition?  
A) Asthma  
B) The common cold  
C) Conjunctivitis  
D) Food poisoning  
 Hint: Symptoms usually clear out in 7-10 days, accompanied by malaise.

rhinitis?

B) It is completely dry  
C) It tends to be more mucopurulent (thick and colored)  
D) It contains bright red blood

produces what?  
A) Eosinophils  
B) Leukotrienes  
C) Bradykinin  
D) Allergen-speciﬁc IgE

chemical mediator?  
A) Histamine  
B) Serotonin  
C) Dopamine  
D) Melatonin  
 Hint: This chemical triggers immediate sneezing, itching, and rhinorrhea within minutes.

histamine release?  
A) Sneezing  
B) Deep, stubborn congestion  
C) Itching (nose and eyes)

15. Which inﬂammatory cells are heavily recruited into the tissue during the 'Late-  
Phase Inﬁltration' (hours later)?  
A) Red blood cells  
B) Platelets  
C) Eosinophils  
D) Neurons  
 Hint: These cells release toxic proteins and leukotrienes, shifting the condition from itching to  
chronic inﬂammation.

A) The patient loses their sense of smell permanently  
B) The nose stops producing any mucus  
C) The patient becomes allergic to new foods  
D) Non-allergic triggers like cold air now cause full symptomatic ﬂare-ups  
 Hint: The ﬁring threshold of the mucosa is lowered, making it overly sensitive to everything.

B) Decreased sense of smell

A) Blindness  
B) Meningitis  
C) Acute or chronic sinusitis  
D) Heart failure  
 Hint: Blocked nasal drainage leads to mucus buildup and infection in the facial cavities.

A) Viruses travel through the bloodstream to the ear  
B) The eardrum ruptures from sneezing  
C) Bacteria enter through the outer ear canal  
D) Blockage of the Eustachian tube causing ﬂuid stagnation  
 Hint: This tube connects the middle ear to the back of the throat and nasal cavity.

B) Asthma  
C) Tuberculosis  
D) Cystic ﬁbrosis

A) Complete loss of hearing  
B) Bleeding from the eyes  
C) Extra stuﬀy nose, breathing diﬃculties, and reduced smell  
D) Growth of hair inside the nose

24. Early-phase drugs for allergic rhinitis primarily target which receptor?  
A) Beta-1 receptors  
B) Alpha-2 receptors  
C) Serotonin receptors  
D) H1-receptors

on day one.

36. Topical decongestants like Oxymetazoline (Otrivin) work through which

decongestants?

up mucus.
