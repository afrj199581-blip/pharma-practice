A) Primary headache  
B) Secondary headache  
C) Cranial neuralgia  
D) Somatic pain  
 Hint: Conditions like migraines and tension-type headaches fall into this self-contained category.

A) Tension-type headache  
B) Trigeminal neuralgia  
C) Migraine  
D) Subarachnoid haemorrhage  
 Hint: This refers to nerve-related head pain aﬀecting the major sensory nerve of the face.  
3. What is the most common type of primary headache disorder?  
A) Migraine  
B) Cluster headache  
C) Tension-type headache  
D) Rebound headache  
 Hint: It causes mild to moderate bilateral pressure, often described as a tight band around the  
head.

B) Pressing or tightening (band-like)

D) 'Thunderclap'

D) Cluster headache

A) 4 to 72 hours  
B) 30 minutes to 7 days  
C) 15 to 180 minutes  
D) 1 to 2 weeks

B) Severe migraine

D) Sinusitis

B) Trigeminal neuralgia  
C) Subarachnoid Haemorrhage

migraine treatment?  
A) Triptans (e.g., Sumatriptan)  
B) Beta-blockers  
C) Tricyclic antidepressants  
D) Opioids

B) Selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonism  
C) COX-1 and COX-2 inhibition

C) Coronary artery disease (CAD)  
D) Type 2 Diabetes

C) They are no longer manufactured  
D) They only work for tension headaches  
 Hint: Triptans are safer, more eﬀective, and have less peripheral vascular toxicity.
