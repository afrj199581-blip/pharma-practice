B) Middle Ear  
C) Inner Ear  
D) Eustachian tube

A) Maxilla  
B) Mandible  
C) Cochlea  
D) Stapes  
 Hint: This bone is also known as the stirrup and helps in sound ampliﬁcation.  
3. What is the primary function of the inner ear?  
A) Earwax production  
B) Pressure equalization  
C) Hearing and Balance  
D) Sound collection  
 Hint: This area houses the cochlea and the semicircular canals.

how long in adults?

ceruminous glands?  
A) Outer 1/3  
B) Inner 1/3  
C) Outer 2/3

tissue?  
A) Cartilage  
B) Muscle  
C) Bone  
D) Adipose  
 Hint: This structure makes it very sensitive to pain and pressure.  
7. Which of the following is a primary protective function of earwax (cerumen)?  
A) Amplifying sound waves  
B) Nourishing the eardrum  
C) Transmitting nerve signals  
D) Trapping dust and repelling water  
 Hint: It acts as a physical and chemical barrier against foreign particles.

pH nature of this environment?  
A) Highly alkaline  
B) Neutral  
C) Slightly acidic  
D) Highly acidic  
 Hint: This pH level is unfriendly to many bacteria and fungi.

wax. What drives this?  
A) Gravity  
B) Epithelial migration  
C) Fluid pressure  
D) Tympanic vibrations

10. Which part of the ear canal is described as being 'very sensitive to pain and  
pressure'?  
A) The pinna  
B) The lobule  
C) The cartilaginous outer 1/3  
D) The bony inner 2/3  
 Hint: Because there is very little subcutaneous tissue between the skin and the bone here.  
11. When taking a patient's history, sudden hearing loss is more likely associated  
with which of the following?  
A) Trauma or infection  
B) Aging  
C) Gradual wax buildup  
D) Genetic disposition  
 Hint: Unlike wax impaction, these events happen instantly or over a very short period.

12. Gradual hearing loss is most characteristically associated with which ear  
condition?  
A) Tympanic rupture  
B) Acute Otitis Media  
C) Wax impaction  
D) Foreign body insertion  
 Hint: It occurs slowly as cerumen builds up and eventually blocks the canal completely.  
13. The presence of mucinous discharge from the ear strongly suggests involvement  
of which structure?  
A) Pinna  
B) Middle ear  
C) Cochlea  
D) Semicircular canals  
 Hint: It is behind the eardrum and lined with a mucous membrane.  
14. Why doesn't mucinous discharge originate from the External Auditory Meatus  
(EAM)?  
A) Because it is too short  
B) Because it is acidic  
C) Because the EAM absorbs mucus  
D) Because the EAM has no mucous glands  
 Hint: The EAM only has ceruminous and sebaceous glands, not mucus-producing ones.

A) Wash hands and explain the procedure  
B) Insert the otoscope immediately  
C) Syringe the ear  
D) Prescribe antibiotics  
 Hint: This is a basic hygiene and patient communication step before touching the patient.

A) Hearing acuity  
B) Eardrum mobility  
C) Tenderness indicating Otitis Externa

A) Use the smallest one available  
B) Use the largest speculum that ﬁts comfortably  
C) Use an adult size for all patients

 Hint: This provides the widest ﬁeld of view while ensuring patient comfort.

adult?  
A) Pull down and forward  
B) Push inward  
C) Pull straight down  
D) Pull up and back  
 Hint: This movement counters the natural S-shape of the adult ear canal.  
19. A healthy tympanic membrane should appear as which color during otoscopy?  
A) Translucent, pearly-gray  
B) Bright red  
C) Opaque white  
D) Dark brown  
 Hint: It should look like a semi-clear, shiny membrane.

eardrum?

A) Cerumen impaction  
B) Otitis Externa  
C) Foreign body  
D) Otitis Media or Mastoiditis  
 Hint: This refers to the bone just behind the ear, which can get infected from a middle ear

22. Redness and swelling of the pinna itself are most likely indicative of what?  
A) Perichondritis  
B) Inner ear infection  
C) Wax buildup  
D) Eustachian tube dysfunction  
 Hint: This is an infection of the tissue covering the cartilage of the outer ear.  
23. Earwax removal is the most common ENT procedure in UK primary care, causing  
which type of hearing loss?  
A) Sensorineural hearing loss  
B) Conductive hearing loss  
C) Mixed hearing loss  
D) Central hearing loss  
 Hint: It blocks sound waves from reaching the eardrum, rather than damaging the nerves.

24. Which of the following is a subjective symptom of cerumen impaction?  
A) Redness of the pinna  
B) Clear visualization of the malleus  
C) A sense of 'fullness' and dizziness

 Hint: These are things the patient feels, not things the practitioner can see.  
25. During otoscopy, what objective ﬁnding conﬁrms wax impaction?  
A) A solid mass obstructing the canal and hiding the eardrum  
B) A retracted eardrum  
C) A perfectly clear canal  
D) A red, bulging eardrum  
 Hint: You literally cannot see past this accumulation of cerumen.  
26. Which type of cerumenolytic works by breaking down the wax into smaller  
fragments?  
A) Olive oil drops  
B) Water-based (Sodium bicarbonate)  
C) Antihistamine drops  
D) Corticosteroid drops  
 Hint: It chemically dissolves and fragments the wax plug.  
27. Olive or Almond oil drops belong to which category of cerumenolytics?  
A) Peroxide-based  
B) Water-based  
C) Acid-based  
D) Oil-based  
 Hint: They act as lubricants to soften the wax for natural migration.

A) Drying it out  
B) Freezing the wax  
C) Releasing oxygen bubbles to disintegrate the mass  
D) Increasing local blood ﬂow  
 Hint: It creates an eﬀervescent (ﬁzzing) action inside the ear.  
29. Before administering ear drops, why should the bottle be warmed in the palms for  
2-3 minutes?  
A) To reach body temperature and prevent dizziness  
B) To activate the ingredients  
C) To sterilize the bottle  
D) To increase its shelf life  
 Hint: Cold liquids hitting the eardrum can stimulate the vestibular system and cause vertigo.  
30. After applying ear drops, how long should the patient maintain a tilted head  
position?  
A) 1 minute  
B) 5-10 minutes  
C) 30 minutes  
D) 1 hour  
 Hint: This allows the drops suﬃcient 'dwell time' to act on the wax.

known as what?  
A) Aviator's Ear  
B) Surfer's Ear  
C) Diver's Ear  
D) Swimmer's Ear  
 Hint: It is often associated with water getting trapped in the canal.

A) Cold, dry winters  
B) High altitudes

B) E. coli and Salmonella

of pathogen?  
A) Viruses  
B) Fungi like Aspergillus  
C) Protozoa  
D) Prions  
 Hint: The suﬃx '-mycosis' indicates this type of organism.

causes:  
A) Increased hearing  
B) Wax production to stop

B) Pain when blinking

Otitis Externa?  
A) It acts as a local anesthetic  
B) It lowers canal pH to inhibit bacterial/fungal growth  
C) It dissolves bone  
D) It paralyzes the tympanic membrane

eﬀective for Otitis Externa?  
A) Steroid + antibiotic  
B) Antihistamine + decongestant  
C) Analgesic + vitamin C  
D) Oil + water  
 Hint: One component reduces inﬂammation/swelling, while the other kills the bacteria.  
39. What strict prohibition should pharmacists advise to patients with Otitis Externa?  
A) Do not eat spicy food  
B) Do not sleep on your back  
C) Do not insert foreign objects like cotton buds into the canal  
D) Do not wash your hair  
 Hint: Nothing smaller than your elbow should go in your ear!

A) 1 day  
B) 2-3 days

41. How does the nature of ear pain diﬀer in Otitis Media compared to Otitis Externa?  
A) It is only felt when touching the ear  
B) It is deep-seated and throbbing  
C) It is sharp and superﬁcial  
D) It only occurs at night

42. In which condition is severe itching (pruritus) very common?  
A) Otitis Externa  
B) Otitis Media  
C) Ruptured eardrum  
D) Mastoiditis  
 Hint: Skin inﬂammation in the canal often presents with this annoying symptom.  
43. Discharge in Otitis Media typically only occurs under what speciﬁc  
circumstance?  
A) If the patient swims  
B) If the patient has a fever  
C) If the eardrum perforates  
D) If the patient uses cotton buds  
 Hint: The ﬂuid is trapped behind a membrane; it can only leak out if that membrane breaks.

medical referral?

B) Mild itching
